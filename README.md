I made a simple game where the player shoots balls at a target and earns a point each time they hit it, with the score shown on screen. 
I believe this qualifies as a game because it has the basic elements of what makes something a game: a clear goal (hit the target), rules that
constrain how the player can act (you can only fire a ball forward, and physics decides whether it actually connects), and a quantifiable outcome
that lets the player track and improve their performance (the score). There's no real-world necessity to throw virtual balls at a virtual target
and the player chooses to take on this made-up challenge for its own sake. Because aim and a bit of randomness affect whether each shot lands, 
success isn't guaranteed, which keeps the challenge meaningful rather than trivial. Even without win/lose conditions or added difficulty,
having a goal, rules, and measurable feedback is enough to make this a game rather than 
just an interactive toy.
